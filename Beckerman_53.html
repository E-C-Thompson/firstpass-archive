4 November 1945 Dear Folks and Ruth: Haven't heard from anyone during the past week. Borrowed a portable typewriter this Sunday morning and am contenting myself with writing a few letters. Normally on Sunday morning when the sun is out we go swimming at the officers club. But this is not type of a day. We had a scare last night when it was announced that a 80 mile gale was going to hit the island. but it never came. That's strong enough to blow all of our tents right off the ground.... Am inclosing some more photos of scenes around manila with short notes on the reverse. This should add a few interesting features to the present scrap book. I would like to see what that looks like... As usual have been doing a bit of reading; worl[illegible] hasn't cut down materially at the office; and intend to start some courses besides my piano lessons. All this takes up time very nicely. Morale jumped a little around these parts this week when the first b[illegible] of troopships arrived here in over three weeks. You probably have read in the papers of what a bad job has been done in getting pacific veterans home. They're far behind schedule in getting the boys home. Bud that sitmation will soon be remedied pretty soon; we hope. I am still feeling in good [illegible] their many boy[illegible] so well py with t wihave had d I feel mpe. We here anven't don quite haphe sh health up, who ha good luck I've had in that respect. All for now folks. Take care. [illegible]
