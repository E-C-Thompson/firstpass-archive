Dear Parent, Ruth, Just am taking a few moment off my lunch hour to drop you these few lines. I just remember that I haven't written for the past few days. Altho eventually I'll probably be writing only once a week. Have you had any luck locating a camera and film for me? Just had a light lunch since I had some sandwiches at tea. Oh by the way I've never told you about the grand tea custom here. No matter where you are or what you doing at 10:15 and 3:15 you stop for what the Army call "Tea-O". (pronounced like Loo). I must admit I've fallen into the habit myself. At the office we all observe tea-time together with the many civilians employed here. Even labor unions when making contracts with employers insist that workers be given two tea-O's a day. Usually have cakes and sandwiches. They call it tea. They resemble our tidbits. I was down to the business the other afternoon at the office of the three career women. Had a little American tea in Japan and I'm sure you can guess what I tell them all is not tea but tea comes in right. That's enough for tea - Jack.
